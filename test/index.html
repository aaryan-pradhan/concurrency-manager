Be careful · timeline

The CV dates are Mar–Jun 2025. The audit, the bug fixes, the benchmark and every speedup number in these answers come from later work, committed in September 2026. If you quote those numbers, say they came from a later rework of the project.

  1. 18 questions · E1–E181 · The engine: ACID, strict 2PL, detection
    Walkthrough and architecture, each letter of ACID and what you actually provide, durability and atomicity, 2PL and its variants, where the code holds locks to commit, why detection, C++ primitives, latches vs locks, and your two best bug stories.
  2. 21 questions · S1–S212 · 2PL, serializability, 1,000+ threads
    Conflict vs view serializability, the precedence graph, the proof that 2PL works, your lock table, waiting, grants, upgrades, how you verified serializability, the 1,000-thread race end to end, data races, CAS, mutex ordering, oversubscription and scaling.
  3. 18 questions · T1–T183 · Throughput speedup and commit rate
    Benchmark method and baseline, where "6x" does and doesn't come from, why each speedup curve has its shape, thrashing, choosing thread counts, noise, sleep-based I/O, comparability, what "100% commit" means and costs, backoff, livelock and fewer aborts.
  4. 19 questions · D1–D194 · Priority, aging, starvation and deadlocks
    Starvation vs deadlock vs livelock, what priority controls, whether aging worked, wound-wait and wait-die, priority inversion, Coffman conditions, prevention vs detection, "eliminated", the wait-for graph and its snapshot, DFS vs Kahn vs Tarjan, victims, detection interval, distributed detection.

How to use this

  • Read the prerequisites, then answer out loud before clicking "Show answer". The written answer is the target. Hit its points in your own words.
  • Every answer starts with its headline in bold. In an interview, say that first, then expand only as far as the interviewer wants.
  • The red "Be careful" boxes matter most. They mark exactly where the CV wording would get you caught, and give a replacement sentence.
  • Numbers come with their conditions. "53.74× at 64 threads with 1 ms of simulated I/O and rare conflicts" survives follow-ups. "53× faster" doesn't.
  • Review links point to the matching section of explanations/ for the full derivation and code.
